Mad Airport Development Test FAQ

Understanding the testing process of Mad Airport bots.

📢 Important Development Announcement

Welcome to the Mad Airport Development Test (MAIDT) FAQ. This page explains how our bot development and testing system works, why we test our bots, and what happens before a bot becomes publicly available.

Our bots are currently being tested by the MAIDT test. This testing process helps us check the quality, reliability, functionality and overall performance of every bot we develop. We want to make sure that our bots are ready for Discord communities and can provide a useful experience for their users.

If a bot successfully passes the MAIDT test, it will be approved for public release. This means that people will be able to discover the bot and add it to their own Discord servers. However, if a bot does not pass the test, it will not be released immediately. Instead, our development team will review the problems, make the necessary improvements, fix reported issues and test the bot again.

Our main objective is not simply to release bots as quickly as possible. We aim to create dependable and properly developed services. Every test result helps us understand what needs improvement. The MAIDT process is an important part of making sure that our bots meet our expected standards before reaching the public.

Thank you for your interest in Mad Airport Development. We appreciate your patience while our bots go through the testing and improvement process.

📚 FAQ – English Levels

Questions and explanations organised from A1 to C2.

A1 – Beginner

A1 Questions

1. What is MAIDT?
MAIDT is the Mad Airport Development Test.

2. Are the bots ready?
No. Our bots are being tested.

3. What happens if a bot passes?
It will become public.

4. What happens if a bot fails?
We will fix it and test it again.

A2 – Elementary

A2 Questions

1. Why do you test your bots?
We test our bots to make sure they work correctly before people can use them.

2. Can I add a bot before it passes?
Public access will be available after the bot passes the MAIDT test.

3. Will failed bots be deleted?
No. They will be checked and improved by our development team.

4. How do you know if a bot is ready?
We check its features and make sure that everything works as expected.

B1 – Intermediate

B1 Questions

1. What is the purpose of the MAIDT test?
Its purpose is to check whether our bots are working properly and are suitable for public use.

2. What happens during the testing period?
Our team checks the bot's commands, features and general performance to identify possible problems.

3. Will every bot become public?
Not necessarily. A bot must pass the required testing process before it can be publicly released.

4. Why might a bot fail?
A bot may fail because of technical problems, incomplete features or other issues that need to be corrected.

B2 – Upper Intermediate

B2 Questions

1. How does MAIDT improve bot quality?
MAIDT provides a testing process through which our development team can identify weaknesses, evaluate functionality and make improvements before public distribution.

2. Does failing the test mean the project is cancelled?
No. An unsuccessful result means that the bot requires further development. The team can investigate the issues, implement corrections and conduct additional tests.

3. What standards are considered during testing?
We consider functionality, reliability, command behaviour, usability and the overall quality of the bot.

4. Why is public release delayed until approval?
Releasing a bot before it is properly tested could create a negative experience for server administrators and members. Testing helps reduce this possibility.

C1 – Advanced

C1 Questions

1. What distinguishes MAIDT-approved bots from development versions?
MAIDT-approved bots have completed the required internal evaluation and have been considered suitable for public distribution. Development versions may still contain unfinished features or unresolved technical issues.

2. How does the development team respond to unsuccessful test results?
Rather than immediately abandoning a project, the team examines the identified shortcomings, determines their causes and introduces appropriate corrections before arranging another evaluation.

3. Why is quality assurance important for Discord bots?
Discord bots often perform tasks that directly affect server management and user interaction. Consequently, consistent functionality and predictable behaviour are essential to maintaining user confidence.

4. What is the long-term objective of MAIDT?
The long-term objective is to establish a consistent development standard that encourages reliability, continuous improvement and responsible public releases.

C2 – Proficiency

C2 Questions

1. To what extent does MAIDT contribute to the credibility of Mad Airport's development projects?
By introducing an evaluation stage prior to public distribution, MAIDT demonstrates a commitment to quality assurance and systematic development. It establishes a distinction between experimental builds and releases that have satisfied the organisation's testing expectations.

2. Why should unsuccessful evaluations be regarded as opportunities rather than setbacks?
An unsuccessful evaluation can reveal deficiencies that might otherwise remain undetected until public deployment. Addressing these deficiencies enables developers to refine existing functionality, strengthen reliability and improve the overall standard of the product.

3. How can a structured approval procedure benefit the wider Discord community?
A structured approval procedure helps establish reasonable expectations regarding the quality of publicly distributed bots. It also encourages accountability within the development process by ensuring that identified issues are considered before a product is made widely accessible.

4. What philosophy underpins the MAIDT development policy?
The underlying philosophy is that public availability should represent the outcome of adequate development and evaluation, rather than simply the completion of initial programming. Continuous refinement, technical accountability and functionality are therefore central to the intended release process.
